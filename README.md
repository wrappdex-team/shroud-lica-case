### What this package is

A shareable, citation-dense case that the linen kept in Turin Cathedral is the burial shroud of the man described in the canonical Gospels as Jesus of Nazareth — a scourged, crucified Jewish male, crowned with thorns, pierced in the side, taken down the same day, and given a hasty linen burial before the Sabbath.

It is **not** a miracle certificate. Science does not “prove” identity across two millennia. What it can do is stack independent physical constraints until the medieval-artist hypothesis and the “any crucified man” hypothesis both collapse under their own weight.

### The one-paragraph case

STURP (1978) established that the body image is not paint: it is a 0.2–0.6 µm dehydration-oxidation of the primary cell wall of linen fibrils, photographically negative, distance-encoded, with no image under the blood. The blood tests as blood. Those facts already rule out every known medieval painting technique. Alternative dating (Rogers vanillin, Fanti FTIR/Raman/mechanical, De Caro WAXS 2022) is compatible with a first-century cloth if storage assumptions hold. The wound pattern — wrist nails, cap-style thorn wounds, un-broken legs, post-mortem side wound with separated blood and watery fluid, hasty incomplete Jewish burial — is the Gospel Passion written in forensic detail. The Sudarium of Oviedo, documented in Spain by 1075, carries a congruent head-wound map and the same reported blood group. No single fact is a proof. The **stack** is the case.
