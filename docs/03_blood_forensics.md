# 03 — Blood and Forensic Pathology

## Blood is blood

Heller & Adler, *Applied Optics* 19 (1980) 2742–2744. Spectroscopic and chemical conversion of heme to a porphyrin. Soret-band absorption 400–450 nm on alleged blood fibrils. X-ray fluorescence: iron concentrated in blood areas, not as a uniform pigment layer across the image.

Heller & Adler, *Canadian Society of Forensic Science Journal* 14 (1981) 81–103. Sticky-tape microchemistry: heme derivatives, bile pigments, proteins. Iron elsewhere on the cloth explained by natural flax processing, not added pigment. No chemical evidence for pigments, stains, or dyes producing the **body image**.

STURP 1981 official line: bloodstains composed of hemoglobin and positive for serum albumin.

## Blood before image

Where blood and body-image coincide, there is no image chromophore under the blood. The blood soaked in, clotted, and shielded the fibrils. The image-forming event came later and stopped at the blood edge. This single observation does two jobs:

1. It kills “the artist painted blood on top of a drawing.”
2. It kills “the image is a scorch that burned through everything including the stains.”

A forger who painted first and added blood later gets this backwards. A forger who applied blood first and then produced a non-contact, distance-encoded, sub-micron image around the stains has invented a process no medieval atelier is known to have used.

## Why the blood is still red

Adler argued high bilirubin from severe trauma, mixed with methemoglobin, keeps archaeological blood redder than expected. This is a **supported chemical rationale**, not a unique proof of Jesus. Any scourged, crucified man in traumatic shock could in principle produce it. It is consistent with the Passion. It is not a name tag.

## Serum halos and clot borders

UV photography shows serum retraction rings around many stains — the behavior of real blood separating into clot and serum on cloth, not painted droplets. Forehead rivulets, the epsilon-shaped forehead clot, nape punctures, and the dorsal flows that follow gravity for a man upright then supine are internally consistent as a two-stage deposition (cross → burial).

## Blood group AB — handle with gloves

Both the Shroud and the Sudarium of Oviedo have been reported as group AB. Ancient-Israel frequency arguments are sometimes piled on top. **Do not load-bear this.**

Kelly P. Kearse (2025, PMC12491371) is the necessary cold water: serological A/B antigens are not unique to human red cells; bacteria and fungi express them; centuries-old cloth is a microbial ecosystem; acquired-B phenomena exist. Antigen reactivity has been reported even on unstained Sudarium areas. Until human-specific identifiers (STR / HLA-quality work under chain of custody) exist, AB is a **soft consistency claim**, not a biometric ID.

## Sudarium of Oviedo

- Size ~84 × 53 cm, plain 1/1 weave, Z-twist.
- Documented in Oviedo by **1075** — two centuries before the 1988 C-14 window.
- Spanish Centre of Sindonology (Guscin; Sánchez-Hermosilla 2015, *SHS Web of Conferences* 15:00007): live-blood AB stains at the nape, puncture pattern consistent with a thorn cap, claimed spatial congruence with the Shroud head after a ~19° rotation.
- Overlay point-counts in the literature range from ~16 carefully measured pairs to popular “120 points” figures. Treat the high counts as **community claims**, not multi-lab bloodstain-pattern-analysis consensus.
- LICA weight: the Sudarium is a **soft historical-forensic amplifier**. If the two cloths covered the same head, the Shroud cannot be a 1350s French invention unless the Oviedo cloth is also a coordinated fraud with a paper trail starting in the 11th century. That is a heavier conspiracy than most skeptics want to carry. It is not, by itself, identity with Jesus.

## Pathology map (body of the Man of the Shroud)

- Scourge marks: hundreds of dumbbell-shaped impressions consistent with a Roman *flagrum*.
- Cap of thorns, not a circlet: puncture wounds around the scalp and nape, not a neat brow ring.
- Nail wounds through the wrists (Destot’s space region), thumbs flexed out of view — matches known nailing mechanics better than the palm-only medieval convention.
- No broken legs.
- Post-mortem wound in the right side; stain chemistry claimed to include separated blood and a clearer fluid (the Gospel “blood and water”).
- No signs of decomposition liquids on the image — short residence time on the cloth.
- Burial: a single large linen sheet, frontal + dorsal, Jewish-context hasty wrapping rather than a completed first-century preparation with washing that would have removed the blood first.

This map is L3. It does not need C-14. It does need the image and the blood to be what STURP said they are.
