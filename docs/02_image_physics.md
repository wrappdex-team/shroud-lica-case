# 02 — Image Physics

This is the load-bearing layer. If the image is a painting, the rest of the case is theatre. STURP spent 120 continuous hours in October 1978 to test that question.

## What the image is made of

Schwalbe & Rogers, *Analytica Chimica Acta* 135 (1982) 3–49:

> the image is the result of some cellulose oxidation—dehydration reaction rather than an applied pigment. The application or transfer mechanism of the image onto the cloth is still not known.

Jumper et al., ACS *Archaeological Chemistry III* (1984):

- Body image and blood are chemically distinct systems.
- Body image = discoloration of surface fibrils, no added material sufficient to explain the image.
- Chemistry consistent with conjugated carbonyl chromophores in aged cellulose.

Official STURP 1981 summary (press conference text, archived at shroud.com/78conclu.htm):

- No pigments, paints, dyes or stains found on the image fibrils.
- X-ray, fluorescence and microchemistry preclude paint as the image method.
- UV and IR confirm those studies.
- VP-8 analysis shows unique three-dimensional information encoded in the image.
- Image of a real human form of a scourged, crucified man. Not the product of an artist.
- How the image was produced remains unsolved.

## Superficiality

Color resides on the crown of the threads, in the primary cell wall, typically cited at **0.2–0.6 micrometres**. The medulla of the fiber is uncolored. A painted film thick enough to be visible to the naked eye is orders of magnitude thicker than this. Any mechanism that soaks, rubs, or thermally chars through the fiber fails this constraint.

## Photographic negative

Secondo Pia’s 1898 plates revealed a naturalistic positive human face when the cloth (a faint yellow-on-linen negative) was inverted. This is not a contrast trick. It is a polarity inversion of spatial intensity. A 14th-century painter working by eye would have had to paint lights and darks backwards and still produce a anatomically coherent positive that no one would see until photography was invented. That is not how workshops work.

## Distance encoding (VP-8 / Jackson)

Jackson, Jumper & Ercoline, *Applied Optics* 23 (1984) 2244–2270:

- Frontal image shading is consistent with a single global mapping from cloth-to-body distance.
- They explicitly tested and rejected, as complete explanations: artist eye/brain/hand, contact-only, diffusion, simple radiation from a body shape, engraving, powder-on-bas-relief, electrostatic imaging.
- Hot bas-relief can mimic distance correlation and some chemistry but fails: one-sided image, observed lateral distortions consistent with a draping cloth, and thermal interaction with already-present thick bloodstains.

This paper is the correct answer to Moraes 2025 (see objections). The “Agamemnon-mask” wrap-distortion problem has been on the table since the early 1980s. The working physical reply is **vertical collimation**: the image-forming process acts along cloth-normals, not as a radial contact print. That is why the face does not smear into a dinner plate when the cloth is flattened.

## UV fluorescence split

1532 Chambéry fire scorches fluoresce under ultraviolet. The body image does **not**. Pellicori, *Applied Optics* 19 (1980) 1913–1920, mapped spectral properties from near-UV to near-IR. The image is a featureless, monotonically increasing absorption toward the blue; contrast at 550 nm is only about 15% against background linen. Lightly scorched areas are spectrally similar but not identical. Air-baking of modern linen plus a latent deposit of perspiration/oils can yield a similar reflection spectrum — which is why STURP left formation open, and why “it looks a bit like a scorch” is not a mechanism.

## Laboratory analog: ENEA vacuum-UV

Di Lazzaro, Murra, Nichelatti, Santoni, Baldacchini, *Applied Optics* 51 (2012) 8567–8578.

- ArF excimer pulses at **193 nm**, nanosecond scale.
- Permanent yellow coloration in a **narrow** fluence window.
- Coloration depth down to the outermost fiber, colorless medulla — the first laboratory match to Shroud superficiality.
- Latent coloration: irradiation that is invisible at first becomes visible after artificial or natural aging.
- Photochemical, not thermal. Does not reproduce 1532-style fluorescent scorches.

Limit: the energy and power density required to color a full body-sized cloth in one event is enormous (Di Lazzaro has quoted peak-power figures in the 10^13 W class). That is not a medieval workshop tool. LICA treats this as **existence proof of a physical coloring channel**, not as a reconstructed resurrection laser.

## What this layer decides

| Paint / dye / stain / tempera | Eliminated |

L1 does not name the man. It names the category: **not art as art was practiced**.
